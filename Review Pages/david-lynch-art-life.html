David Lynch: The Art Life

Year: 2016

Director: John Nguyen, Rick Barnes

Reviewed: 08/03/2026

#: 2

"David Lynch: The Art Life" is a documentary about David Lynch and the Art Life.

I'm going to endeavor not to make this "Joey yaps about how much he loves David Lynch." Because I love his work. I find myself undergoing a sort of hypnosis whenever I watch one of his films, spellbound in a particular way unique to him. I pour over his interviews like scripture. I have to try hard not to put him on a pedestal. You can't put people up on pedestals. You just can't. But know I am writing this review from within a tornado of obsession.

The documentary itself is pretty standard fare. Lynch sits in his Los Angeles compound recounting stories starting in his childhood up until the completion of "Eraserhead". His narration is accompanied by footage of him working in his workshop, along with sparse photographs and film of him in his youth.

The filmmakers also added in images of his paintings to punctuate each story. The chosen paintings do seem to be interpretations of that moment in Lynch's life although I don't know that they always match up perfectly. It does feel like there was some attempt to mimic Lynch's own style in certain editing decisions. I get the instinct, but the effect doesn't really work. Lynch's films have this style he's cultivated that goes along with the story. Having eerie drones over archival footage doesn't have the same affect.

I think it also suffers from being really focused on Lynch, on Lynch's interpretations of his life. When he says his family pleading with him to get a job, "just didn't get it" we know that he eventually did succeed in making a career out of his art. They didn't know that. He had a child, you know? I don't think it's intentionally disingenuous. I think they wanted to make a movie about one of the great artists of our time, and the framing is him telling the stories so it favors his POV. That's fine, just something I can't help thinking about anytime I look at biographical work.

My complaints said, Lynch is good at telling stories. He has an interesting mind, and it's cool to see more of who he was. I enjoyed myself.
