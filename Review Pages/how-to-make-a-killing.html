How to Make a Killing

Year: 2026

Director: John Patton Ford

Reviewed: 07/20/2026

#: 3

'I had fun' is often deployed as a kind of backhanded compliment in movie reviews. It implies that a movie was enjoyable in some way that is lesser. It was funny, or the action set pieces were good, but it's not REALLY a great film. You don't see a lot of reviews calling "Au hasard Balthazar" fun.

I try not to use the phrase that way because I really respect fun movies. On any given day, the first two Gremlins movies could sneak their way into my top four. It takes some skill to be fun. I think it's like telling a good joke or writing a catchy song, deceptively difficult.

I guess what I'm trying to get at is that "How to Make a Killing" is fun, and it's not great, but one does not follow from the other.

Great casting, first of all. Glen Powell is a fun guy to watch. Jessica Henwick manages to bring some heart to a charecter who wasn't particularly well written. I feel bad, but Zach Woods and Topher Grace should do nothing else but play spoiled rich kids. Margaret Qualley is over the top and goofy(positive). I guess it might not be great acting, but every time she showed up on screen, my wife and I were cheering. I mean, she's standing in for the concept of greed, you want her to ham it up a little.

It's refreshing just how evil the movie let its villains be. I think it's good we've entered into a world where villains and monsters get more rounded writing, but sometimes it's nice to just have a genuinely evil guy in a movie, y'know? We don't always need a tragic backstory. Sometimes you can write a guy who just wants power and is bad.

One of my problems with the movie actually is how much it tries to make its protagonist more likeable. We get voiceover explaining that actually he felt bad about killing this guy or doing that, something that smells to me like studio interference. I think there's a more thrilling version of the movie where his feelings are more ambiguouis. Maybe they could have even managed to string us along into believing he's kind of a good guy, only for his eventual turn to be played for comedy. Look just how evil he is, how stupid could you be? He did it all for the money kind of thing, but that would require a big rewrite.

I'm not sure if Ford wanted it to be more ambiguous and that's why the story kind of swung back and forth between Becket being a merciless killer who cares for nothing but money and a weird robot boy trying to make things work. As it is, it feels less like he's a really deep characters and more like he's two different very shallow characters vying for screentime.

Also, it should have had a little more fun with the killings. More "Hitman" style looney tunes murders please!
